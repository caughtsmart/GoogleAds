## ⚠️ Recommendation: switch Brand Search to PHRASE match

The escalation trigger set on 7 Aug — generic share above 50% for two
consecutive days — has been met, and spend has now broken its budget.

| Day | Spend | Brand clicks | Generic clicks | Generic % of spend |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 Aug | £14.99 | — | — | — |
| 8 Aug | £18.24 | 40 | 28 | **58%** |
| 9 Aug | **£26.85** | 50 | ~45 | **65%** |

Yesterday's £26.85 is **134% of the £20 budget**, and spend has risen
three days straight (£14.99 → £18.24 → £26.85). Only 50 of 117 clicks
were actual brand queries. The rest went to the generic dice/D&D/40k
market: "dice shop", "custom dice", "metal dnd dice", "giant d20",
"blood bowl", "kill team", "ork starter set", "chessex dice" — plus two
competitors ("noble knight games uk", "hobbyworkshop"). **Not one
generic term converted on either day.**

**The fix: change the "Loaded Dice" keyword from BROAD to PHRASE.**
Phrase match keeps every brand query — "loaded dice", "loaded dice
barry", "loaded dice shop", "loaded dice uk" all contain the phrase —
while eliminating the entire generic category in a single change, rather
than adding negatives for every dice term that exists.

Expected effect: spend back to roughly £7-10/day of pure brand defence,
saving ~£15/day. Those generic products are ones Loaded Dice sells, but
PMAX already serves those queries — paying £0.30/click for them out of
the brand-defence budget is the expensive route to the same customer.

This is a spend-and-search-term judgement, deliberately independent of
the unreliable revenue data.

I can apply it on approval (remove the broad keyword, push the phrase
one), or it is a 30-second change in the UI.
