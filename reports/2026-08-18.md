## 🔄 Correcting Sunday's tracking conclusion

On Sunday I said tracking had "settled at 15-18%, treat as the new
normal". **That was measuring the wrong thing, and it was too
pessimistic.** Tracked share has a moving denominator, and store revenue
grew sharply — so a falling share did not mean falling attribution.

Google's *absolute* attributed revenue, which has no denominator problem:

| Period | Tracked £/day | Shopify £/day | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-incident (2-3 Aug) | £670 | £1,698 | 39% |
| Trough (5-12 Aug) | £137-£330 | ~£2,100 | 6-15% |
| **Now (14-17 Aug)** | **£558** | £2,653 | 21% |

Absolute attribution is down only ~17% on the pre-incident baseline,
while store revenue is up 56%. The trough was real; the "stuck at 15-18%"
plateau was largely arithmetic.

The clincher is the last two days, which happened to have revenue very
close to the pre-incident baseline:

| Day | Shopify | Tracked | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16 Aug | £1,434.79 | £561.32 | **39%** |
| 17 Aug | £1,432.40 | £456.53 | **32%** |

On like-for-like days, tracking is back in the 30-40% band.

**Status of the strict test:** 16 and 17 Aug are in band but not yet
matured (maturation only adds value, so they can only improve); 15 Aug
matured at 21% on a high-revenue day. So the three-consecutive-matured-day
rule is not formally satisfied. Given I have twice called this early, I am
not declaring it met — but the underlying picture is materially better
than Sunday's report implied, and **the PMAX budget decision looks likely
to become answerable within a couple of days.**
