## 🔍 The AOV drop is explained — it is product mix, not discounting

Third consecutive day under £30 (£23.38 → £25.78 → **£22.93**) against a
£40-50 norm, on entirely normal order volume. Top sellers of the last
three days:

| Product | Orders | Avg line value |
|---|---|---|
| MTG: The Hobbit Play Booster Pack | 10 | £11.88 |
| The Wicked and the Warped (Hardback) | 9 | £20.36 |
| Fallout Funko POP! Deathclaw | 5 | £11.80 |
| MTG Marvel Play Booster Pack | 4 | £5.41 |
| Mystery Dice Goblin Christmas Dice Bag | 3 | £8.25 |
| Final Fantasy TCG Opus 29 Booster | 3 | £17.45 |

Almost the entire top ten is **single booster packs and small
collectibles** — £5-20 items — rather than boxes, army sets or sealed
cases. That is a mix shift, not a discount, and it is consistent with the
TCG wind-down: as sealed boxes leave the catalogue, what remains selling
is loose singles and small accessories.

**Why this matters for the ads.** If AOV settles around £23-25, every
click is worth roughly half what it was, so the CPC the account can
afford falls with it. Two things soften that for now:

- PMAX's CPC has already fallen to **£0.27** (from £0.31-0.41 a week
  ago), so it is naturally buying cheaper traffic.
- Brand Search runs at £0.15, well inside any sensible ceiling.

**No action needed yet** — the account is already trending the right way
on cost per click. But if AOV holds at this level once the wind-down
settles, the £0.30 Brand Search ceiling and PMAX's budget both deserve a
fresh look against the new basket economics. I will raise that as a
proper recommendation at the end of the seven-day watch (day 3 of 7)
rather than reacting mid-transition.
